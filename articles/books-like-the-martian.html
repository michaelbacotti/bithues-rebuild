Books Like The Martian — The Ultimate Comparison Guide

Articles

The Martian redefined what survival science fiction could be. Andy Weir gave us Mark Watney — a botanist stranded on Mars, presumed dead, with no way home — and then made us care about soil pH and potato farming in a way no book had before. If you finished The Martian and thought "I want more of that," this guide is for you. We have organized twelve comparable reads into segments so you can find exactly what you are looking for.

Best for Survival Problem-Solving

These books give you the same intellectual rush as watching Watney solve problem after problem. Every crisis has a real solution.

Weir's follow-up to The Martian — a lone astronaut wakes up on a mission he does not remember with an alien partner he needs to trust. The science is just as rigorous, but the partnership at the center gives it emotional dimensions that The Martian only gestures at. Essential reading for anyone who loved the first book. Bradbury's Mars is a fading dream, and his colonists are haunted by what they have left behind. The problem-solving survival spirit is there, wrapped in some of the most beautiful prose the genre has produced. See Price on Amazon.

Best for Mars-Focused Reads

Want to stay on the red planet? These books explore Mars from angles The Martian did not cover.

Robinson's monumental trilogy spans centuries of Martian colonization. Where Weir keeps you in one astronaut's head, Robinson zooms out to show an entire world being transformed. Epic in every sense. See Price on Amazon.

Roach's account of the real engineering and psychological challenges of sending humans to Mars is the best companion piece to The Martian you could ask for. She covers everything from astronaut toilets to zero-g intimacy. See Price on Amazon.

A coming-of-age story set against the backdrop of Martian independence. Bear combines planetary-scale science fiction with intimate personal narrative. See Price on Amazon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Should I read Project Hail Mary before or after The Martian? They are independent — either order works. Most people read The Martian first, then move to Project Hail Mary.

Q: What if I want something as funny as The Martian but not as science-heavy? Try The Collapsing Empire or The Murderbot Diaries. Both have protagonists with Watney-level wit who solve problems through intelligence.

Q: I have read everything Weir wrote. What's next? The Expanse series or Children of Time — both keep the science honest while delivering the epic scale. For the most similar problem-solving thrill, Seveneves is the serious answer, though it is much denser.

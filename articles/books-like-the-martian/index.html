Books Like The Martian -- The Ultimate Comparison Guide

Lists

The Martian redefined what survival science fiction could be. Andy Weir gave us Mark Watney — a botanist stranded on Mars, presumed dead, with no way home — and then made us care about soil pH and potato farming in a way no book had before. If you finished The Martian and thought"I want more of that," this guide is for you. We have organized twelve comparable reads into segments so you can find exactly what you are looking for.

Why The Martian Works

The thing that makes The Martian compelling is not the science per se — it is the fact that every problem is solved through process. Watney does not have moments of inspiration that save him. He has hours of systematic work that save him, most of which fails. The reader experiences the accumulation of small efforts rather than the flash of a single idea, and that accumulation is what makes the eventual success feel earned in a way that pure narrative luck never could.

What Weir understood that many science fiction writers miss is that the audience for hard science fiction does not actually want the science to be simplified. They want the science to be explained clearly enough that they can follow it. The Martian gives you every equation, every calculation, every reasoning step — and trusts you to keep up. Most of the time, you can. That experience — of being trusted by the book to be smart enough — is why readers finish The Martian feeling like they could solve problems on Mars if they just had enough duct tape.

The books on this list give you that same feeling in different configurations. Some match the engineering rigor. Some match the humor. Some match the lone protagonist against impossible odds. None of them are copies — they are the books that draw from the same well of wonder at what human intelligence can accomplish when it refuses to accept the impossible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Should I read Project Hail Mary before or after The Martian?

They are independent — either order works. Most people read The Martian first, then move to Project Hail Mary. If you loved The Martian's problem-solving more than its humor, read Project Hail Mary sooner rather than later.

Q: What if I want something as funny as The Martian but not as science-heavy?

Try The Collapsing Empire or The Murderbot Diaries. Both have protagonists with Watney-level wit who solve problems through intelligence rather than equations.

Q: Is Artemis by Andy Weir worth reading?

Yes — it is a different kind of book (crime thriller on the moon rather than survival on Mars), but Weir's voice and scientific accuracy are present throughout. The protagonist's sarcastic humor carries the narrative.

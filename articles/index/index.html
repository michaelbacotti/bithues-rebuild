The Articles section is a collection of essays, analyses, and observations on reading, science, fiction, and the spaces in between. Here you'll find thoughtful pieces on the nature of reading itself, practical guides to getting more from your time with books, and explorations of ideas that live at the intersection of what we read and how we live.

How this section is organized

The articles are organized chronologically by publication date, with the most recent pieces appearing at the top of the index. Within the chronological ordering, articles cluster by topic — pieces about reading and reading habits appear together, pieces about specific genres or authors appear together, pieces about the publishing industry appear together. The clustering is not strict; readers looking for specific topics can use the search function rather than scrolling the index.

The full list below includes a one-line summary of each piece and a link to the full article. The summaries are written to give the reader enough information to decide whether to click through to the full piece; they are not teasers but are accurate representations of what the article covers. If a piece is part of a series, the series is noted in the summary and the reader is pointed to the other pieces.

What we publish here

The articles section covers four overlapping areas: reading and reading habits, the publishing industry, the nature of fiction and literary forms, and the intersection of books with other cultural forms (film adaptations, television series, video games, podcast discussions). The four areas overlap; a piece about the television adaptation of a science fiction novel touches reading, publishing, fiction, and cultural adaptation simultaneously. The articles section reflects this overlap rather than partitioning the topics cleanly.

We publish new articles at the rate of roughly two per month, with additional pieces added when we have something substantive to say. The publication cadence is not fixed because the editorial judgment about whether a piece is ready is more important than the calendar. A piece that needs another month of revision will not be published to keep the schedule; a piece that is ready will not be held back to wait for the next scheduled slot.

How to use the articles section

Readers who want to work through the articles in order should start at the most recent piece and work backward, since earlier articles are referenced in later ones when the references remain current. Readers looking for specific topics should use the search function on the site or the topic tags at the bottom of each article. Readers who prefer to read by series rather than chronologically should follow the series links in the relevant articles.

The articles section is supported by advertising and by an optional paid newsletter subscription. The advertising does not influence the editorial content; the editorial content does not change based on whether the reader is a paid subscriber or a free reader. The site's revenue model is documented on the About page.

Editorial process

Articles in this section follow the same editorial process as reviews: drafting by the assigned editor, technical or editorial review by a second desk member, and a final pass by the editor-in-chief before publication. The articles typically take longer to produce than reviews — two to four weeks from initial outline to publication, versus one to two weeks for a review — because the topics often require more primary research and the arguments need more careful construction.

Series

Some articles are part of series. Series are noted at the top of the first article and at the bottom of each subsequent article in the series. The series index is maintained on this page; readers who start with a later article in a series can navigate back to the earlier articles through the series links. When a series concludes, a final article summarizing the series is published and linked from each article in the series.

Reader feedback

Reader feedback on articles is welcomed through the contact form. Substantive feedback — corrections, alternative interpretations, additional sources — is read by the editorial team and often leads to follow-up articles or to corrections on the original piece. Letters that warrant a public response are published in the next article in the relevant series, with the writer's permission.

Article archive

The full archive of articles published on this site is available on this page, organized chronologically with the most recent pieces at the top. The archive goes back to the site's launch and includes every article we have published. Articles that have been updated significantly are noted with an update date at the top of the article; the original publication date is preserved at the bottom of the article for the historical record.

Readers looking for a specific topic should use the search function rather than scrolling the archive. The search function indexes all article titles, subtitles, and the first 200 words of each article; full-text search of the archive is available through the site's search interface.

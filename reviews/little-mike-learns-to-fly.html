Little Mike dreams of flying. With friends John and May, he meets Pilot Thomas who teaches the wonders of airplanes.

Little Mike: Learns to Fly by Michael Jr takes the Little Mike character from the beach and puts him in the cockpit of a different kind of curiosity. Here, Little Mike's imagination is directed upward: he wants to fly, and through a chance encounter with Pilot Thomas, he gets an introduction to how airplanes actually work.

The book is honest about the fact that it is introducing real concepts—lift, drag, the four forces of flight—but it does so through dialogue and illustration rather than through explanation. Pilot Thomas is a good character: knowledgeable without being condescending, and genuinely delighted by the questions children ask. Little Mike and his friends John and May are curious and persistent, which makes them believable as children who would actually retain what they are being shown.

The book has a warmth that makes it work as both entertainment and elementary STEM introduction. It does not feel like educational material dressed in a story's clothing—it feels like a story that happens to include accurate information.

Recommended for children ages 4-8 who are interested in how things work, particularly machines that fly.

Book Review December 2025

Veins of the Earth

by Fictional Author | 388 pages | Horror / Scientific Fiction

The cave system beneath the Patagonia plateau was supposed to be a four-week survey. Dr. Elena Vásquez and Dr. Tom Marchetti had mapped cave networks in Iceland, in Laos, in the karst formations of southern China. This was supposed to be a routine assignment — a graduate student's thesis site, a paper on speleothem formation rates, a break from the administrative burden of their respective departments.

The first sign that something was wrong came when the radiocarbon dating started returning impossible numbers. The sediment layers didn't match the geological record above them. The cave was older than the mountain it sat inside — older, the datings suggested, than the limestone itself.

Deep Time

The book's central achievement is its treatment of geological time as a narrative force, not just a backdrop. Vásquez and Marchetti are trained to think in millions of years, but the novel makes that abstract quantity viscerally real by showing what human objects look like against it — a brass compass from the 1930s, buried in sediment that predates the first Homo sapiens by two hundred thousand years. The horror isn't that the cave is old. It's that it's old in a direction that shouldn't exist.

The writing refuses to lean on genre conventions. The tension comes from professional competence meeting something it cannot account for — the scientists' confidence in their instruments and methods is the thing that makes the revelations land. When the Geiger counter starts returning numbers that have no possible explanation, Vásquez's first instinct is to recalibrate it. That instinct, and what happens when it doesn't help, drives the book's second half.

Verdict

Veins of the Earth is a serious novel that happens to be terrifying. It's interested in what it means to encounter something genuinely beyond understanding — not a monster to be survived but a fact to be survived with. One of the stronger debut horror novels in recent memory.

Book Review January 2026

Rules of Survival

by Jorak Veldt

Adventure

Rules of Survival by Jorak Veldt is the first book in what appears to be an ongoing sequence set in the Paleolithic. The premise is simple and powerful: what if the survival protocols that kept early human bands alive were learned, passed down, and paid for in blood?

The book's protagonist is a boy in a band of early humans. The narrative follows his education in the band's laws: when to speak, how to allocate water, what to do when a fire dies. These are not abstract rules. They are physical knowledge encoded in the body's responses, and the book makes you feel the cost of learning them wrong.

Veldt writes with anthropological rigor and narrative urgency. The prehistoric world is rendered with sensory precision — you can feel the cold, smell the smoke, hear the watch-towers. The character's interiority is necessarily limited by the setting, which is actually a strength: Veldt doesn't pretend to know what Paleolithic consciousness was like, and the restraint makes the book more honest than it might otherwise have been.

Rules of Survival is a strong entry in the growing body of rigorous prehistoric fiction. It is both a good story and a good piece of science-informed historical fiction.

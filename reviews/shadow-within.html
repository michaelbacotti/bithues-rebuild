Book Review January 2026

The Shadow Within

by Elena Maris

Self-Help

Elena Maris's The Shadow Within is a grounded, psychologically informed guide to shadow work — the practice of identifying and integrating the parts of one's psyche that have been disowned, suppressed, or otherwise kept out of conscious awareness. Maris approaches the subject without the mystical framing that often accompanies shadow work in popular spirituality, while still acknowledging the genuine depth and emotional complexity that the work involves.

The book's greatest strength is its clarity of language. Shadow work is a concept that accumulates jargon easily, and Maris takes care to define terms precisely before using them. This might seem like a minor virtue, but in a genre where imprecise language often masks imprecise thinking, it makes a meaningful difference. Readers who have bounced off other shadow work books because of vague or confusing terminology will find this one more accessible.

The exercises range from quick daily practices — moments of self-observation that take a few minutes — to deeper journaling work that requires sustained attention over weeks. Maris is careful to distinguish between the two, which helps readers calibrate their engagement appropriately. The safety warnings around deep shadow work are also notable: Maris explicitly addresses what can go wrong when the work is pursued without adequate support, which is a responsible stance.

The main limitation is that The Shadow Within does not break new theoretical ground — it is a practical synthesis rather than an original contribution to the psychology of the shadow. But as a practical guide for self-help readers who want depth without being overwhelmed, it is the most usable book in this category that I have encountered. Maris has a gift for naming psychological patterns precisely enough that readers can recognize them in their own experience, which is the foundation of any useful shadow work.

The book's approach to journaling is worth highlighting separately. Maris does not prescribe a single journaling method but instead offers several frameworks — some readers will respond to structured prompts while others will prefer open-ended reflection. By presenting multiple approaches rather than insisting on one, the book accommodates different working styles, which increases the likelihood that readers will actually stick with the practice.

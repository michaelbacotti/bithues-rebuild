Behind the designs

A short note on three of the pieces we get asked about most often — what they are, where the ideas came from, and what to look for if you are deciding between variants.

Orion Nebula — printed from a Hubble palette most people never see

The Orion Nebula is one of the most photographed objects in the night sky, but the colors you see in our canvas are not the colors your eye would register at the eyepiece. They are the colors the Hubble Space Telescope captures when its filters isolate specific wavelengths of light — sulfur, hydrogen, oxygen — that the human retina cannot distinguish on its own. What looks like a photograph is closer to a map: each color is a question being asked of the gas cloud. We chose this composition because it gives a wall print something most space photography does not — a reason to be looked at twice. The first time you see it, you see fire. The second time, you see information.

The cat astronaut helmet tee, and why transparent PNGs print best

Most of our apparel designs use transparent PNG backgrounds rather than the white-box look you see on cheaper print-on-demand shops. The reason is simple: a transparent design lets the garment color become part of the composition. The cat astronaut helmet design looks completely different on a Solid Black tee (full night-sky feel, the helmet glows) than on Heather Grey (softer, more editorial, almost vintage children's-book). On White, it becomes a clean line drawing suitable for design-minded friends. Same artwork, three very different garments. If you have ever wanted a tee that looks like it was chosen, not just ordered, this is the kind of detail that makes the difference.

Why a ceramic mug is the right place for the moon

We almost made the cat-on-the-moon design into a t-shirt first. Then we thought about who the cat belonged to, and where that person would want to see her in the morning. A mug is the answer. A mug is a small, daily companion. The cat on the moon looks up at you while you drink your coffee, and then she is there again at 3pm when you sit down with tea. She is the sort of small, slightly absurd comfort object that book people and cat people have always been drawn to — the same energy as a literary cat mug in a used bookshop window. Glaze is glossy, the print wraps the mug fully (no white stripe down the side), and it is dishwasher and microwave safe.
